# Statement of Purpose — MBZUAI M.Sc. in Artificial Intelligence

My goal is to develop artificial intelligence systems that remain reliable when they move beyond controlled demonstrations and encounter long inputs, incomplete evidence, ambiguous language, and changing real-world conditions. I am particularly interested in machine learning, natural language processing, information retrieval, and evaluation methods for agentic systems. The M.Sc. in Artificial Intelligence at Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence would allow me to deepen the mathematical and research foundations behind the systems I have begun building and to learn how to turn engineering observations into rigorous research questions.

I graduated from Alexandria University with a B.Sc. in Computer Science, specializing in Intelligent Systems, ranked first in my class with a CGPA of 3.9738/4.00. My coursework and independent work led me from core machine learning and neural networks toward a broader question: how can an AI system produce useful results while preserving the evidence, state, and evaluation needed to understand when it is wrong? This question now connects my work in language systems, computer vision, generative media, and robotics.

My principal current project, SAGA, is an agentic narrative-intelligence platform designed to transform long-form books and series into evidence-linked representations of characters, dialogue, events, relationships, state, and time. Building SAGA has required more than connecting a language model to a prompt. I have designed durable jobs and runs, structured tool contracts, hybrid retrieval, provenance-aware outputs, and bounded model use. I also established evaluation contracts rather than judging progress through isolated examples. Current measured evidence includes deterministic quote detection at 0.8563 F1 and a BookNLP event-trigger challenger at 0.7791 F1. Importantly, I keep experimental challengers separate from adopted defaults until evidence justifies the change. This work has shown me how much stronger applied AI becomes when its architecture makes failure observable and its claims measurable.

I have pursued the same reliability-oriented approach in other domains. In RenderLab, an invite-only AI image and video creation platform, I integrated cloud-hosted generative-media workflows with durable finalization, retries, cancellation, failover, owner-scoped access, and guarded release qualification. In computer vision and robotics, I built an ESP32-based six-servo biped robot controlled through a Python application and Streamlit diagnostics dashboard. The system combines live camera input, color-based robot and ball tracking, pose calibration, and autonomous movement until a collision-distance threshold is reached. I also developed VisionDeck, a computer-vision suite for face landmarks, hand tracking and gesture recognition, YOLO object detection, and webcam or IP-camera input. These projects taught me to treat models as components within larger systems whose data, latency, uncertainty, and physical or user-facing consequences must be considered together.

My professional and teaching experience has strengthened my ability to explain technical ideas and build disciplined learning workflows. As an AI Subject Matter Expert at Kayfa, I authored implementation-focused lessons, assignments, and Python notebooks covering machine learning, neural networks, convolutional networks, clustering, anomaly detection, and unsupervised learning. At Armstrong, I built AI-assisted content pipelines and developed material for a national technology-education program. As an AI and Robotics Instructor with Engineering for Kids-Egypt, I guided students through Python, C++, Arduino, circuits, and robotics projects. Teaching repeatedly tested whether I understood a method well enough to explain its assumptions, failure modes, and practical use.

At MBZUAI, I want to study machine learning and language-centered AI with a strong emphasis on evaluation and trustworthy system behavior. I am interested in research on evidence-grounded generation, retrieval under long and heterogeneous contexts, structured reasoning with tools, and evaluation methods that expose semantic and temporal failures rather than hiding them inside aggregate scores. I would also like to explore how these methods transfer across multimodal and embodied settings, connecting my experience in natural language systems with computer vision and robotics. My objective is not merely to use larger models, but to understand which combinations of learned models, deterministic structure, retrieval, and explicit constraints produce systems that are both capable and accountable.

MBZUAI is especially attractive to me because it is dedicated to artificial intelligence and brings together advanced study, active research, and an international technical community. I would contribute a record of academic excellence, independent system building, measurable evaluation, and experience communicating AI concepts to different audiences. In return, I hope to gain deeper research training, close mentorship, and the theoretical tools needed to design stronger experiments and publish work that advances reliable applied AI.

After the M.Sc., I intend to work as an AI researcher and engineer on systems where language models, retrieval, perception, and software infrastructure must operate together. Over the longer term, I want to lead research-driven engineering that makes advanced AI systems more evidence-grounded, testable, and dependable. I see MBZUAI as the right environment to turn my current applied foundation into rigorous research capability and to prepare for that responsibility.
